\section{Details of the live evaluation}
\label{app:live}

This appendix holds the detail behind Chapter~\ref{ch:live}.

\subsection{Sampling of the held-out sets}
\label{app:live-sampling}

A sampling script drew the held-out sets with the fixed seed 249053, stratified over 24 topic groups of the archive, sampling 304 and 289 emails for the first two sets. The topic groups only spread the samples over all topics and play no part in the results. The script excludes every email already used, rows with privacy signals, duplicates, rows marked unusable, and questions shorter than 40 or longer than 1,500 characters. The archive was already anonymized, and two language-model agents removed any names and signatures that were left. Rows that were not student questions were dropped: 33 of the 304 in the first sample, 14 of the 289 in the second, and five of the final 303. The third set consists of the 298 emails that remain of the 303 eligible after the first two draws.

\subsection{Judge sensitivity}
\label{app:live-grader}

The original Opus gradings of the same answers gave 76.3 percent correct and 2.3 percent wrong on holdout 1 (older rubric). On holdout 2 they gave 78.6 and 3.2 percent for run $a$, and 80.5 and 2.4 percent for run $b$. Sonnet gave lower correct rates by 5.8, 1.6 and 3.2 points. The first difference partly reflects the rubric change. On holdout 2 the two judges agreed exactly on 449 of 550 answers (81.6 percent) and on the distinction between correct and not correct for 484 of 550 (88.0 percent). On holdout 1 the figures are 203 of 271 (74.9 percent, including the effect of the rubric change) and 228 of 271 (84.1 percent). The same answers can also be graded differently in different judge batches. The Stage~10 run scored 84.0 percent when graded side by side with Stage~11, against 81.7 percent when graded alone.

\subsection{Deterministic check of stated details}
\label{app:live-check}

Judges were unreliable for invented details (nine of 22 rows at Stage~10, see Chapter~\ref{ch:live}). A separate checking script therefore compares every date, amount, URL and email address in an answer against the question, the full tool output and the extra files of confirmed facts. It matches dates across formats (ISO, day-month, Macedonian and English month names, ranges) and amounts across number formats. After one data fix for a guessed announcement URL, it flagged only computed sums, dates taken from the question and weekday conversions on the earlier recorded runs. On the two runs of holdout 3 it flagged seven and six rows, plus four and three rows with only relative dates. Every flagged detail was worked out from the sources and not invented. Examples are fee totals added up from FAQ parts and the run date itself. The check also exposed the broken announcement URLs (Section~\ref{sec:live-ab}).

\subsection{Latency}
\label{app:live-latency}

Sequential runs at Stages~9 and 10 gave a median time to first token of 5.0 to 5.4 seconds (90th percentile 10.7 to 13.6 seconds). Concurrent runs make this figure larger, so they are not used for latency.
